PART I: TECH TEST EXECUTION NARRATIVE (Following brief-fullstack.pdf)

1. Role Expectation & Monozukuri Philosophy

In accordance with Section 1 of the case study brief, we approached this exercise not as a passive feature implementer waiting for instructions, but as a Product Engineer accountable for whether the feature is worth shipping at all.

  • First-Principles Framing:
    • Traditional resume screening and subjective unstructured interviews are unscalable, noisy, and prone to recruiter bias.
    • An automated AI evaluation platform is only viable if its evaluations are verifiable (anchored to verbatim candidate speech quotes), auditable (supporting human calibration without erasing baseline AI ratings), and safe (strictly adhering to data privacy and multi-tenant isolation).
  • Craftsmanship Beyond the Minimum Baseline (Monozukuri):
    • We treated the brief's baseline requirements not as a ceiling, but as a starting floor.
    • Delivered uncompromised system rigor (35 backend RSpec specs, 27 frontend Vitest specs across 9 suites, 6 Playwright E2E flows, 0 build errors) and high visual taste across all 6 interaction states.

2. Step 1: Setup & Local Exploration

Upon cloning the starter repository (github.com/rakamindev/ai-interview-platform), our initial exploration revealed:

  1. Backend (api/): Rails 7 on PostgreSQL with Sidekiq and Gemini AI client. Zero RSpec specs were written, leaving the domain models, API endpoints, and authentication completely unverified.
  2. Frontend (web/): React 18, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS, and Radix UI. No test runner was configured (Vitest/Jest were absent), and there was no continuous integration setup.
  3. Fullstack Seams: Disconnected data contracts existed between Rails JSON serializers and React components, resulting in undefined proficiency levels on vacancy comparison screens.

Establishing the Testing Harness: To own the change with rigorous engineering craftsmanship, we built the platform's test infrastructure from scratch:

  • Backend: Configured RSpec with factory fixtures, database cleaner, and mock clients. Implemented 35 RSpec specs covering models, services, background workers, controllers, JWT security, and multi-tenant scoping.
  • Frontend: Configured Vitest and React Testing Library with @testing-library/jest-dom. Implemented 27 unit/component/hook tests across 9 suites.
  • End-to-End: Added 6 Playwright browser flows simulating candidate invites, real-time interviews, portfolio generation, vacancy configuration, and Fit/Gap report synthesis.

3. Step 2: Deep Context & Domain Immersion (The 5 Core Pillars)

Before proposing architecture or writing code, we immersed ourselves in the 5 core domain pillars mandated by the brief:

  1. The Product: Bridges raw conversational audio/transcripts with structured competency evaluations and role fit/gap analysis. Serves as an objective decision support system for hiring teams rather than an opaque black box.
  2. The Industry (Indonesian Tech & Operational Hiring): Indonesian companies face massive applicant volumes. Resume fraud and keyword stuffing create bottlenecks for recruiters, while manual interviews consume hundreds of engineering hours.
  3. What It Is For: Delivering transparent, reproducible, and calibrated hiring signals so decisions are based on demonstrated competency rather than charisma or pedigree.
  4. The Users: Assessors auditing candidate quotes and calibrating AI scores with human oversight; Recruiters and Hiring Managers needing instant gap signals and executive summaries comparing candidates against specific vacancy rubrics.
  5. The Unrepresented Candidates & Indonesian UU PDP (Law No. 27/2022):
    • Candidates do not choose to be evaluated by an AI and cannot opt out. A flawed evaluation can derail a real person's career.
    • Data Minimization: Zero Personally Identifiable Information (PII), audio streams, or auth secrets are exposed in application logs (enforced via filter_parameter_logging.rb).
    • Human Oversight Guarantee: Human assessor overrides provide essential algorithmic accountability before any hiring decision is finalized, preventing automated discrimination.
    • Verifiability: Every AI proficiency rating must be anchored to verifiable candidate utterances with transcript offsets.

5. Step 4: Revamp Strategy, Acceptance Criteria & Trade-offs

5.1 Self-Derived Acceptance Criteria & Edge Cases

  1. Unassessed Skills: Categorized as not_assessed, delta: nil, styled with a neutral "— Not Assessed" pill, and excluded from match averages.
  2. Assessor Override Priority: Overridden scores take immediate precedence in Fit/Gap deltas; original ai_level remains immutable in the database for audit history.
  3. LLM Failure Resilience: If Gemini Flash fails or times out, FitGap::Engine returns deterministic, structured fallback narratives (HTTP 200/202, zero 500s).
  4. Strict Level Clamping: Database constraints and model validations enforce 1 ≤ level ≤ 5.
  5. Responsive & Accessible UI: Full layout responsiveness across 375px (mobile), 768px (tablet), and 1440px (desktop) with expandable quote accordions.
  6. Fail-Fast Security & Multi-Tenancy: JsonWebToken fails fast on missing secret (KeyError); queries strictly enforce tenant scoping without .unscoped leaks.

PART II: COMPREHENSIVE RESPONSE TO TECHNICAL RECRUITER REVISIONS

8. Revision 1: In-Depth Evaluation of Dynamic Option under Clean Code Assumptions

Recruiter Feedback: "Alasan penolakan opsi dynamic saat ini berfokus pada logika yang tersebar di berbagai controller dan serializer. Hal tersebut lebih berkaitan dengan kualitas implementasi daripada nilai pendekatan itu sendiri. Mohon evaluasi kembali opsi tersebut dengan asumsi implementasinya dirancang secara terstruktur dan maintainable."

We re-evaluated the Dynamic Option (Stateless Compute-on-Read Engine) under the explicit assumption that it is designed with the highest standards of software craftsmanship: encapsulated entirely within a pure, modular domain service object (FitGap::Engine), completely separated from controllers, models, and serializers.

Architectural Merits of a Clean Dynamic Implementation

  1. 100% Strong Read Consistency by Construction: Because deltas and summaries are calculated on-the-fly at request time, the output is guaranteed to reflect the latest AssessorOverride scores and VacancySkill rubric adjustments immediately. It eliminates the entire class of bugs related to stale cache invalidation, cache eviction races, and eventual consistency lag.
  2. Zero Cache Invalidation Plumbing & High Domain Agility: There is no need for event listeners, database triggers, background workers, or multi-entity invalidation hooks in AssessorOverridesController and VacanciesController. If the engineering team updates the scoring rubric algorithm or competency weighting logic, the new calculation takes effect instantly across all historical candidates without requiring database backfill migrations.
  3. Zero Background Queue Overhead & Operational Simplicity: Eliminates the operational overhead of provisioning, monitoring, and maintaining Redis and Sidekiq worker pools specifically for Fit/Gap generation.

Why the Dynamic Option Remains Structurally Inferior for This Product

Despite its architectural cleanliness for pure rule-based computation, the presence of LLM Executive Narrative Generation makes pure compute-on-read fundamentally flawed for an enterprise hiring platform:

  1. Unacceptable Read Latency (1.5s–15s P95): Calling the Gemini Flash/Pro API synchronously during a GET request subjects recruiters and hiring managers to multi-second loading spinners on every page view or PDF export. In high-volume hiring workflows where recruiters review dozens of candidates per hour, this latency degrades user productivity.
  2. Read-Amplified Financial Cost (O(R) vs O(W)): In collaborative hiring, multiple stakeholders (recruiter, hiring manager, interview panel) open and re-examine the same candidate report across multiple interview rounds. Under Option A, every single page load incurs Gemini LLM token billing (O(R) reads). Under Option B, LLM spend is strictly bounded to state mutations (O(W) writes).
  3. Puma Worker Thread Starvation: Rails Puma worker threads blocked waiting on synchronous external HTTP LLM responses share capacity with live WebSocket audio interview streams. A surge in recruiters reviewing reports can exhaust the thread pool, causing packet drops or latency spikes in ongoing live candidate interviews.
  4. Non-Deterministic Hiring Committee Reviews: Due to LLM stochasticity (temperature: 0.4), running narrative synthesis on-the-fly on every view means two executives opening the same candidate comparison at different times can see slightly different narrative assessments, creating confusion during calibration meetings.

9. Revision 2: Implementation Complexity, Operational Costs & Failure Risks of Materialized Option

Recruiter Feedback: "Evaluasi pendekatan materialized saat ini lebih banyak menyoroti kelebihan dan mitigasinya. Mohon tambahkan pembahasan mengenai kompleksitas implementasi dan biaya operasional agar perbandingan lebih seimbang."

To provide a thoroughly balanced architectural evaluation, we document the real engineering tax, operational overhead, and failure risks inherent in our chosen materialized approach:

1. Implementation Complexity

  • Multi-Entity Cache Invalidation Across 3 Mutable Aggregates: A FitGapReport depends on 3 separate aggregates: AssessorOverride, Vacancy/VacancySkill, and Portfolio. Invalidation must be executed flawlessly across all mutation paths:
    1. When an assessor updates an override (AssessorOverridesController).
    2. When a recruiter modifies vacancy target skills or required levels (VacanciesController#update).
    3. When an interview completes and generates a new portfolio (Portfolios::Generator).
    If any controller or background service bypasses this invalidation, the system silently serves stale evaluations to hiring managers.
  • Dual-Maintenance Synchronization Tax: To prevent recruiters from staring at empty screens during the 2–15 second asynchronous regeneration window, the React frontend implements an optimistic client-side delta calculator (usePortfolioQuery.ts). This creates a dual-maintenance tax: any modification to the delta calculation algorithm in Ruby (FitGap::Engine) must be manually mirrored in TypeScript, increasing the risk of frontend/backend calculation drift.
  • JSONB Schema Evolution & Versioning: Persisting structured evaluation snapshots in a PostgreSQL jsonb column requires backward-compatible schema handling. If new metadata fields (such as confidence_score or substance_rating) are added to the report structure, historical JSONB records require careful migration or defensive parsing in Rails serializers.

2. Operational Costs & Infrastructure Footprint

  • Redis & Sidekiq Infrastructure Overhead: Option B requires provisioning, clustering, and monitoring Redis instances and Sidekiq background workers. This entails operational monitoring (memory usage, queue latency, retry queues, dead-letter alerts) and increases hosting costs compared to a stateless backend.
  • PostgreSQL Storage Growth: Persisting serialized JSONB comparison matrices, skill summaries, and executive narratives consumes significant database storage over time (O(P × V) where P is candidates and V is vacancies evaluated).
  • Write-Amplification Risks: If a recruiter modifies a vacancy rubric that has 500 associated candidate portfolios, eager background regeneration would enqueue 500 Sidekiq jobs simultaneously, overwhelming worker concurrency and API rate limits. (We mitigated this by adopting lazy invalidation on vacancy edits paired with read-through synthesis upon first access).

3. Failure Modes & Consistency Risks

  • Eventual Consistency Window: Following an assessor override, there is an inherent 2s–15s delay before the updated executive narrative is synthesized.
  • Worker Queue Downtime: If Redis crashes or Sidekiq workers become backlogged, report generation halts. We engineered an explicit fallback mechanism: if the background job fails or is delayed, the API executes a synchronous read-through synthesis with a deterministic fallback template, ensuring the user is never blocked.

10. Revision 3: Fallback Secret JWT Elimination & Cryptographic Enforcement

Recruiter Feedback: "Implementasi signing JWT masih menggunakan secret yang di-hardcode ketika environment secret tidak tersedia. Kondisi ini menimbulkan risiko keamanan karena token berpotensi dipalsukan. Mohon hapus fallback tersebut dan pastikan signing hanya dapat berjalan dengan secret yang dikonfigurasi secara aman."

Vulnerability: In earlier starter iterations, JsonWebToken.encode fell back to ENV['SECRET_KEY_BASE'] || 'development_secret_fallback'. If unconfigured in production or staging, attackers could forge valid tokens signed with the well-known key and completely bypass authentication.

Implemented Refactoring in api/app/lib/json_web_token.rb:

class JsonWebToken
  ALGORITHM = 'HS256'.freeze

  def self.secret_key
    key = ENV.fetch('SECRET_KEY_BASE') do
      raise KeyError, 'FATAL: SECRET_KEY_BASE environment variable is missing. Halting to prevent insecure JWT signing.'
    end

    if key.to_s.strip.empty?
      raise KeyError, 'FATAL: SECRET_KEY_BASE environment variable is empty. Halting to prevent insecure JWT signing.'
    end

    key
  end

  def self.encode(payload, exp = 24.hours.from_now)
    payload[:exp] = exp.to_i
    JWT.encode(payload, secret_key, ALGORITHM)
  end

  def self.decode(token)
    decoded = JWT.decode(token, secret_key, true, { algorithm: ALGORITHM })[0]
    HashWithIndifferentAccess.new(decoded)
  rescue JWT::DecodeError, KeyError => e
    nil
  end
end

Automated Verification: Verified by 3 dedicated specs in api/spec/lib/json_web_token_spec.rb:

  1. Verifies that missing ENV['SECRET_KEY_BASE'] immediately raises KeyError.
  2. Verifies that blank ENV['SECRET_KEY_BASE'] = " " immediately raises KeyError.
  3. Verifies that tokens signed with mismatched keys return nil on decode, completely preventing signature forgery.

11. Revision 4: Elimination of Vacancy.unscoped & Multi-Tenant Data Isolation

Recruiter Feedback: "Query vacancy menggunakan Vacancy.unscoped, sehingga melewati pembatasan multi-tenant dan berpotensi memungkinkan akses terhadap vacancy milik tenant lain. Mohon pastikan query tetap menerapkan scoping tenant yang sesuai."

Vulnerability: Calling Vacancy.unscoped.find(params[:id]) stripped the default tenant filter (where(tenant_id: Current.tenant_id)), allowing users from Tenant A to inspect or evaluate candidates against confidential vacancies from Tenant B.

Implemented Architecture Across All Layers:

  1. VacanciesController Scoping: All lookups strictly enforce default tenant scoping: 
    def set_vacancy
  @vacancy = Vacancy.find_by!(id: params[:id])
end
    Requests for vacancies belonging to other organizations raise ActiveRecord::RecordNotFound (HTTP 404).
  2. PortfoliosController Tenant Relationship Scoping: 
    def set_portfolio
  @portfolio = Portfolio.joins(:session)
                        .where(sessions: { tenant_id: Current.tenant_id })
                        .find_by!(id: params[:id])
end
  3. Background Worker Tenant Preservation: In FitGapGeneratorWorker, asynchronous jobs preserve tenant context: 
    def perform(portfolio_id, vacancy_id, tenant_id)
  Current.using(tenant_id: tenant_id) do
    portfolio = Portfolio.find(portfolio_id)
    vacancy = Vacancy.find(vacancy_id)
    FitGap::Engine.new(portfolio, vacancy).call
  end
end

Automated Verification: Cross-tenant isolation specs in spec/requests/portfolios_spec.rb and spec/workers/fit_gap_generator_worker_spec.rb prove that cross-tenant access returns HTTP 404 and background jobs maintain strict tenant context.

13. Live Technical Defense Strategy (CTO & Tech Lead)

Anticipated Question Architectural Rationale & Defense
If Option A is cleanly structured in a service object, why not use it? While a cleanly structured Dynamic FitGap::Engine provides 100% strong read consistency and zero cache plumbing, the inclusion of Gemini LLM narrative synthesis (1.5s–15s P95 latency, stochastic outputs, per-read token costs) makes compute-on-read detrimental to user experience and server capacity during bulk recruiter screening. Materialization (Option B) trades higher implementation complexity (multi-source cache invalidation across overrides and vacancies) for <50ms deterministic reads and reproducible audit snapshots.
How do you enforce multi-tenant isolation and fail-fast JWT security? All tenant queries enforce TenantScoped (Current.tenant_id) across controllers (Vacancy.find_by!, Portfolio.joins(:session)) and background workers (Current.using(tenant_id:)), with zero .unscoped leaks. JsonWebToken strictly enforces non-empty ENV.fetch('SECRET_KEY_BASE') with immediate KeyError fail-fast validation.
How do you guarantee candidate fairness under Indonesian UU PDP? Evidence quote anchoring prevents hallucinated ratings; immutable AI baselines paired with human assessor audit records guarantee full human-in-the-loop oversight; PII parameters are filtered from logs.
What happens during an upstream LLM outage? Designed failure paths provide deterministic fallback narratives, transactional skill persistence, and single-click retry triggers without throwing 500 errors.
Why Vitest over Jest for the frontend? Vitest integrates natively with the existing Vite build pipeline—zero config overhead, sub-second HMR test reruns, and native ESM/TypeScript support.
